# Pi Pocket features

The full tour. For setup, see the [README](../README.md).

- **Durable runs.** Every model request, tool call, queued message, and subagent is a durable task in one SQLite file. Kill the server mid-run and start it again: the run continues. A tool call that was cut off reruns if it is safe to; otherwise the model is told it was interrupted.
- **Appearance.** One look, after Claude Code's desktop app: warm paper in light mode, warm charcoal in dark, with Claude's clay as the accent. Appearance → **Mode** picks **System** (the default: light or dark as the system is, and it follows a change at once), **Light**, or **Dark**, per browser; `/theme light`, `/theme dark`, `/theme system`, and the launcher's Themes do the same. A reload paints the last mode before the first frame, so it never flashes the other one. Appearance also sets the conversation's **Text size** (any from 10 to 32 px), where PiP opens, and how the conversation shows thinking, activity, and quotes. Motion follows the system's reduced-motion setting.
- **Sheets and controls.** Sheets have a 56px title bar on wide screens, a back chevron when opened from another sheet, grouped inset lists, round toggles, and segmented settings. On phones they keep their bottom-sheet grip and drag-to-close gesture. Text sheets have Cancel and the main action together at the bottom; all existing keyboard and back flows stay available. Buttons, fields, and search share the same rounded controls across the app.
- **The sidebar.** One column, after Claude's desktop app. At its top are its places: **New session** (Alt+N), **Search** (the launcher, Ctrl/⌘+K), extensions' places (Projects, Design), **Artifacts** (every session's), **Scheduled** (the running board with its Scheduled filter on), and **Running now** (with how many sessions work, and a red mark when a subagent waits for you or failed and nobody has dealt with it yet). Running now has two views, switched in its head: **Sessions** (the running board) and **Subagents** (the subagents board). Then PiP and your projects, then the session list. At its foot are you, Devices, Providers (for the owner), and Appearance. The list shows only the sessions people started: threads and helpers that another session started (Agent Teams, a project's threads) are reached from where they were started. The list has Priority, Recent, Folders, and Archived (an icon) in a segmented switch; sessions grouped by day or by folder, in groups that fold; and pinned sessions on top (per browser). **Priority** (since 0.27.0) groups sessions by what they ask of you, not by when they ran, and has no Pinned group: pinned sessions go by their state. **Needs you** holds sessions with a call waiting for approval (the longest wait first), then failed runs not seen yet, then sessions with failed subagents nobody dealt with; its tab carries a warning-colored count of them, and its head offers **Dismiss N failed** when more than one failed. **Ready to read** holds sessions that finished since this browser looked, that are marked unread, or that have new chat (newest first; **Mark all read** in its head, and a button on a row's hover). **Working** holds sessions where Pi or its subagents work, a background command runs, or a request waits for a provider's limit (a clock pill says when it clears). **Quiet** holds the rest, folded at first. Each row is one line. A call's row has a **Review** button, which opens the call in the row (one at a time): the tool and its whole command, why the guard asks, and how long ago, with Deny and Allow. Allow shows only when the command is whole in the row (80 characters, one line); otherwise Deny and Open. An extension's question can be declined from its row, or opened to be answered where it waits, a person who may not allow what Pi did for someone else cannot allow it, and only people who can steer answer. After an answer the next call opens by itself with the focus on its Review, and the answered row glows in the group it moves to; an answer cannot be undone, and says so in a notice without Undo. A failed run's row has a ✕ that dismisses it (the run counts as seen, like opening it) and a failed subagent's ✕ deals with it, as the subagents board does; Dismiss and Mark all read say so in a notice that undoes them, except a subagent's dealt-with failure. The open session's row stays in its group while it is open, though opening it reads it, and moves when another session opens. Needs you shows six rows and a **Show N more** button. A search lists the matches, as on the other tabs. The tab a browser starts on is the one it kept (Recent when it kept none). Each session is one line: a square in its folder's color whose shape says where the session is (stepping quadrants while Pi or its subagents work, two bars while a call waits for you, a cross when its run failed, solid when it finished since this browser last had it open or is marked unread, and hollow once read or never run), its title (semibold with new chat, when marked unread, or when open, and the open one on the accent's soft fill), and on the right a small pill with ⑂ and how many subagents work there (or failed with nobody dealing with it yet, in red), who else is there, a pulsing dot while Pi works or a "needs you" pill while a call waits for you, a dot for new chat (or for a session this browser marked unread), and how long ago; its folder and model are in its tooltip. Hover a row to archive, pin, or mark it unread; on a touch screen, hold a finger on it (the row shows the press, and moving the finger scrolls instead) for a sheet of its actions; with a mouse, right-click a row for the same actions in a small menu at the pointer (Shift+right-click keeps the browser's own menu, and Ctrl+click on a Mac still selects). They are: open it in a new tab, copy its link or its folder's path, rename it, fork it (a new session with its whole history, through Pi's latest finished reply), start a new session in its folder, share it (an invite to only that session), pin or unpin it, move a pinned one to the top or bottom of the pins, mark it unread or read, extensions' actions, select it, and archive or unarchive it. Pinned sessions keep the order they were pinned in, or moved to. Marks are per browser, like pins (a session's square counts as read here when this browser opens it, or has it open in sight as its run ends; a failed run's cross stays until its next run starts, and a restart forgets it), and opening the session clears its mark; the session's menu marks the open one unread and goes back to the list. A session moves up the list when it is renamed, gets a message, or Pi starts work there for any other reason (a scheduled message, a subagent's report, a message sent again after a restart), so the one that ran last is on top. Archiving says so in a notice that undoes it. Ctrl/⌘+click selects sessions, Ctrl/⌘+drag selects every row it passes (the list scrolls at its edges), and Shift+click selects a run; on a touch screen, after **Select**, taps select more rows or let them go. A bar under the list archives, unarchives, or pins them, and Esc (or back, after Select) lets go. Archiving does not move a session in the list, so one brought back returns to where it was. Drag its edge to resize the sidebar (to 40% of the screen at most); a double click puts it back. On phones the drawer (Ctrl/⌘+B on a narrow window) and the list with no session open are the same column. On wide screens with no session open, the home screen shows a fastfetch-style summary, buttons for a new session, the launcher, and Appearance, and the sessions to jump back into.
- **The side dock.** Beside the conversation on wide screens, one panel at a time, under a row of tabs, as in Claude's desktop app: a project's **Threads** (beside its coordinator and threads), **Files**, **Changes**, and **Browser**; × closes it. On a phone each panel covers the conversation, with Back.
- **Session cleanup** (since 0.30.0, the owner's alone). **Remove and clean up** on an archived session (a held row, a right-click, or selected rows) takes it out of the app for everyone and removes Pi Pocket's own files of it: its worktree (its branch stays), its uploads, and its background logs. Its schedules, background tasks, PR watch, webhooks, repository watches, published artifact links, modules' public page links, and session lessons end too. The confirm says what goes and its size first. The transcript stays in Pi Pocket's database until Pi Durable can remove a conversation; Pi Pocket keeps a list of removed sessions for that. A session where Pi works, or whose worktree has uncommitted changes, stays, and says why; a fork's uploads stay while a fork still shows them. Menu or the launcher → **Clean up archived sessions** looks at the archived sessions not used for 7, 30, or 90 days (or any age): **Remove worktrees** removes their worktrees and keeps the sessions, and **Remove N sessions…** removes them, after the same confirm. A removal cut short by a restart goes on at the next start.
- **Launcher.** Ctrl/⌘+K (on a touch screen, the **Launcher** tile among the places, or the ⌘ button in the session search) opens a rounded search window for sessions, actions, and themes, with fuzzy matching. Search-type pills sit above grouped results with folder-color tiles, action icons, or four-color theme swatches; the selected row has a soft fill. **All**, **Sessions**, **Actions**, and **Themes** select the search type without clearing your words; `>`, `@`, and `#` still work. All starts with six recent sessions; **Show all** searches the whole session list, including archived sessions. Result counts show when a list is capped. Titles and folder or command details highlight matches, and sessions show their current, working, waiting, or archived state in a pill on the right. Arrowing onto colors shows them at once; Enter keeps them, Escape goes back. A theme that changes the look shows once chosen. Arrows, Tab/Shift+Tab, and PageUp/PageDown move the selection; Enter opens it. **Close**, Escape, a tap outside, or Back closes the launcher. Each opening starts with a fresh search and a focused input, even when it reopens during its fade-out. On a phone it is a sheet from the bottom edge, like the app's other sheets: drag it down to close it. On a touch screen it opens without the keyboard, so the results stay in sight; a tap on its search brings the keyboard up.
- **The menu.** The top bar's **⋯** opens a 360px popover below it on wide screens, and a bottom sheet on phones. Three columns of rounded place tiles open Files, Changes, Browser, Artifacts, Chat, Find, Launcher (on touch screens), Branch, PiP, and Peeks when available; badges show counts or news, and open places are tinted. Session actions, Subagents, and App rows have right-aligned hints and separate groups. App includes **Keyboard shortcuts** (`?`). Arrows, Home/End, and Tab move through the controls; Esc, Back, Close, or a tap outside closes the menu. Back from a sheet it opened returns to the menu.
- **Keyboard.** Alt+1…9 jumps to a session (hold Alt to see the numbers), Alt+↑/↓ goes to the previous or next one, Alt+N starts a new one (where Ctrl/⌘+1…9 starts it in a favorite folder), Alt+B opens or closes the browser, Alt+E the Files tile, Alt+T turns Show thinking on or off, Alt+V starts or stops dictation, and `?` lists every shortcut. On a computer, a session opens with the caret in its message box, ready to type; on a touch screen it waits for a tap, so the keyboard does not cover the conversation, and on a Mac a session opened with Alt+1…9 or Alt+↑/↓ leaves the box alone, so the next Alt shortcut still works (Option types characters in a box).
- **The conversation.** A column of turns, made for phones. Your messages are cards with the sender's avatar (the accent for yours, the sender's color for someone else's), who sent each and when; Pi's replies are plain prose at Appearance's text size, streamed as they come (lists keep a quiet marker column; code, tables, and rich blocks are rounded cards). The **Message time** item under completed replies shows the time, with the date for earlier days, and can be hidden or moved in Appearance → Layout. What Pi does between its words, its thinking and tool calls, folds into one line that says what it did and for how long ("Thought, searched, read 2 files, ran 1 command · 18s"), and opens, as a card, into a timeline: a line per call with a circle for how it went (✓ done, × failed or denied, ! waiting for an approval, a spinner while it runs, ⑂ for a subagent, ✦ for a thought) and what it came to (lines added and removed, lines read, matches, tests passed, an exit code, "needs approval"), and tapped, its input and output, with the diff viewer's diffs for edits (line numbers, syntax colors, and the words that changed) and bash's live output. The newest turn's groups are open and earlier ones closed, unless Appearance's **Keep activity folded** (off by default) keeps them on their line until you open one; a thought alone still follows Show thinking. An approval is a card at the end of its turn (the command, the reason, Deny and Allow; once answered, the call's line says who allowed or denied it), and while Pi works one line, a pulsing dot with the state in the accent (the warning color while it waits for an approval), says what it does (thinking, working, waiting for an approval, retrying) and for how long. With **Show thinking** on in Appearance (or Alt+T, or the launcher), the current thought shows above that line as it streams, and the thoughts in the conversation show their words once written (a thought alone in its group opens, and one on a timeline opens its step). A chevron before the line's dot folds the live thought away, and again brings it back. Alt+T shows a short on/off notice, even while Pi is idle. During tool calls the latest thought stays; a new response clears it until the model shares new thinking. Models that share none say so, rather than showing an old thought as current. A fold holds in that session, also over a reload, until you open it again; the setting is off until you turn it on, in each browser, and while it is off or folded the live thought is neither selected nor drawn. The reading area follows new words until you scroll up in it, and follows again for each new thought. While Pi works, **Steer**, **Queue**, or **Interrupt** beside the model chip says whether a message steers the run, waits for it to end, or stops the run at once (the tool call too) and is sent first (on a narrow message box, such as a phone's, they are one button with the chosen mode that opens a menu of the three, so the model chip stays in view; since 0.29.0), while the queued messages stay in order with their senders and modes; **Stop** stops the run and holds the queued messages (since 0.28.1): a bar above the message box says how many, **Send** sends them in their order as the people who wrote them, and **Show** lists them, each with **Edit** for its sender and a × to withdraw it (anyone their own, the driver anyone’s), while subagents’ reports, and PiP’s queue, are still withdrawn; Interrupt goes back to Steer after one use, or when Pi stops before it was used, while Steer and Queue stay as chosen; Ctrl/⌘+Enter sends a message the other way while Pi works (queued when Steer is chosen, steering when Queue is) and leaves the choice as it was, and on Interrupt or with nothing running it sends as Enter does (since 0.28.0); queued messages can be withdrawn, and your own, tapped open, can go back into the message box to change (**Edit**: its words above what you were typing, on a line of their own, with its files), move to Queue or Steer until Pi reads it, or go now as an Interrupt (a second tap confirms; `CONFIRM_INTERRUPT` in `web/composer.js` turns that off), with these buttons in a quiet row at its right (since 0.28.0): that stops the run and sends only that message, while every other queued message, from everyone, stays queued in its order with its sender and mode (a restart in between loses or repeats none), and so do subagents' reports; with take turns on, moving one to Steer or Interrupt waits for your turn at the wheel, as sending does; Stop (or Esc twice) stops the run. A run stopped in the middle of a tool call ends with a "Stopped." line under its card. A session you go back to shows at once where you were in it, as this browser tab kept it, and what came while you were away rises in: at the bottom the conversation glides up to it, and scrolled up the row you read stays where it is. With Appearance's **Wait for updates** (off by default), a session that changed while you were away shows only once its updates have come (or after 2.5 seconds). Scrolled up, a round button above the end of the message box goes back down to the newest message.
- **Getting around on a phone, with one hand.** Back (the swipe, Android's back button, the browser's) goes back to where you were: it closes what opened last (a sheet, the menu's next sheet back to the menu, a file back to the Files tree, back to Changes when the file was opened from there, the Files tile or the Browser panel, the drawer) before it leaves the session, and leaving goes to the session before or to the list, scrolled and searched as you left it. A session opened straight away (a link, a notification) has the list under it, so back does not leave the app. The top bar's menu starts with what opens beside the conversation (Files, Changes, the Browser, extensions' panels), a row each. On the conversation, swipe left for Files and right to go back; in Files, swipe right to go back. Each swipe shows a mark that lights when letting go will act. The top bar leads back to the list (counting other sessions that wait for an approval) and keeps only the buttons with something to say. Every control is a thumb's size on a touch screen (small ones, such as a code block's Copy or a reply's Pin, keep their look and take a wider tap); a tap anywhere on the message box, off its one line of text, still goes to the text; a quick second tap does not land on whatever just slid in under it; tables in replies scroll sideways instead of crushing their columns; a diff's header keeps the file's name on a line of its own on a phone The Files tile and the browser slide in as screens of their own and slide away on back, a file slides in over the tree, and the browser's tools sit on their own row under the address. Drag a sheet down, or the drawer left, to close it. In an iPhone home screen app, which has no back gesture of its own, swipe in from the left edge to go back.
- **Reply to a passage.** Select words in one of Pi's replies, on a phone or a desktop, and a **Reply** button appears by the selection (below it on a touch screen, where the system's menu sits above). It puts the passage in the message box as a Markdown quote (up to 1,500 characters), right after what is there, with the caret on the line right under it, ready to type; Reply again stacks another quote, so one message can answer several passages, each with its own words. In the box each quote is one piece: drawn as one block apart from your words, its empty lines in it (Appearance → **Quotes in the message box**: **Fade**, the default, a tint that fades out to the right; **Tint**, a soft block; **Ghost**, faded words only), with a × on its first line, and a "2 quotes · Clear quotes" line above the box. Typing inside a quote writes on the line under it instead; Backspace on the line under a quote turns it red, and a second Backspace removes it; a reload keeps the quotes and their pieces. The box knows each quote by where it is, so one typed by hand ("> …"), even with the same words, is plain text; undo after removing a quote brings it back as one. The message goes to Pi as written, with a blank line set around each quote so Markdown keeps it apart; a paste placeholder or an `@file` inside a quote stays the passage's words (nothing expands or goes along). It shows in the conversation with each quote as a quote block where it was written. **Separate**, the fourth choice there, keeps quotes out of your words instead: each Reply adds a strip over the box (× on each, **Clear quotes** with more than one, and a reload keeps them), and the next message goes with every strip's passage quoted first, in order, then your words. With PiP on, PiP's orb (**Ask PiP**) sits beside **Reply** in the same pill: it opens PiP with the passage in a quote strip over its box, ready to type (as a board, Appearance → PiP → Board, the quote strip of the board's message box); your message goes with the passage quoted first, and shows with it as a quote block. PiP also gets, unseen by people, which session the passage came from (its number, title, and folder: `about` on the submit request, a `<pocket-context>` part after the message that no view or resend shows), so it can read that session for more. In Appearance, **Reply to a passage** and **Ask PiP about a passage** (both on by default) each turn their own button off in that browser; with both off, nothing shows.
- **Forks.** Tap Pi's reply → More → **Fork from here**: a new session with everything up to that reply, which then goes its own way. Tap one of your messages to **edit it and send it again**, or send any message again, to the same model or another one (a retry). Each makes a new session that forks just before the message; the original stays as it is. A fork gets its own chat (with the pins of what it inherited), a link back to where it came from, and optionally a git worktree of its own.
- **A new context.** Menu → **New context** (or `/reset [note]`): Pi starts fresh and no longer sees the messages so far, while everyone still does. An optional handoff note carries over what matters.
- **Memory.** Short lessons Pi keeps across sessions: how to build and test a project, a gotcha it hit, a convention, a preference someone stated. Pi proposes one with its `memory` tool (one or two sentences each, never secrets) only when a person asks for it, or when Pi is sure that it will help in many later sessions, never for a one-off detail, and proposes fixing or forgetting one that turns out wrong; the owner approves. What Pi reads (a web page, a post, a comment, a file) could talk it into a "lesson" that would reach every later session, so nothing Pi saves, changes, or forgets counts until the owner says: **Approve** and **Discard** show right under Pi's memory call, with the words that wait and where they would apply (this project, or everywhere: every session and PiP), and in the sheet. Approve approves only what you saw: if Pi changed it since, the app says so and shows the new words. Lessons and project names are kept on one line, without invisible characters, so Pi reads only what the owner saw. A change or forgetting Pi proposes leaves the lesson as it was until then. Lessons you add yourself count at once. Each is for this project, for everywhere, or for this session alone: a project's lessons reach every later session in the same repository, its worktrees included (or the same folder, outside a repository), everywhere's reach every session and PiP, and a session's own reach that session and its subagents for the rest of its life, through compaction and restarts, and no other session (since 0.29.0; Pi uses `scope: "session"` for something to keep only here). A fork starts with its session's lessons in force, as its own; what waits for approval stays with the session it was saved in. Archiving a session keeps its lessons for when it comes back. In PiP the same part is **PiP only**: lessons for PiP that no session gets. Menu → **Memory** (its hint says how many apply here, everywhere, and in this session) opens as a wide sheet; a switch at its top, **This project**, **Everywhere**, or **This session**, each with how many it holds and how many of them wait for approval, picks which it lists and where a lesson you type in goes. Each shows who saved it and a link to the session it came from (when you may see it), with Edit and Forget; type one in to add it by hand, or use `/remember run the tests with make check` or **Remember…** on a message. A guest's lessons wait for the owner too, and a guest's change to one in force is a proposal they can withdraw. What waits shows at the top of the owner's sheet; the menu's hint says how many. Lessons waiting take no room from the approved ones, and a person (or Pi for them) may have 20 waiting at most. Only the owner, or whoever saved a lesson, changes or forgets it; viewers only read. A project keeps 40 lessons, everywhere 30, and each session 20 of its own, each in a room of its own; past that, forget one first. A person's session lessons that wait count in that session only (20 at most there, and 40 in all sessions), not toward the 20 waiting elsewhere. A server from before 0.29.0 uses a session's lessons nowhere (it shows one that waits in the owner's queue, for "session “…”"), never as lessons for everywhere. Changes show as lines in the chat. Turn it off in Extensions.
- **A lean prompt.** A new session gets only the tools most conversations use. `browser`, `subagent`, `background`, `schedule`, `watch_pr`, `ask_someone`, and the design tools wait, and so do their prompt sections; the prompt lists them with a line each, and Pi loads one with `tool_search` when a task needs it. On a model whose provider takes tool changes in the messages (Claude 4.8 and 5, and later), the tool joins the list and stays for the conversation; on any other, Pi calls it from a `codemode` script, so the tool list and the prompt cache never change. Long guides, such as the design guide and the page-to-app messages of artifacts, are files that Pi reads when it needs them. Turn the **Tool search** module off in Menu → Extensions to offer every tool every time. A test keeps Pi Pocket's own part of a plain session's prompt under 14,000 characters (about 5,000 tokens: schemas and this prose run at about 2.75 characters a token), so it does not grow by accident.
- **Plan mode.** The **Plan** chip by the message box (or `/plan`): Pi reads files, runs read-only commands, and uses tools their extensions mark read-only, then proposes a plan; writing, editing, and other commands are blocked. Subagents inherit plan mode and explore under the same restrictions. **Approve plan** turns plan mode off and tells Pi to go ahead; it waits until Pi has answered since plan mode went on.
- **Scheduled messages.** `/schedule in 2h check the deploy`, `/schedule tomorrow 9am …`, or `/schedule every weekday 8:00 summarize CI`, or `/schedule every 30m between 9:00 and 17:00 on weekdays check the queue`, or Menu → **Scheduled messages** (also the violet pill on the left of the status line, which shows how many are set and, hovered, counts down to the next): Pi gets the message at its time, in this device's time zone, even with nobody watching, and the people in the session get the usual "Pi finished" notification. Pi can schedule its own follow-ups with its `schedule` tool. Since 0.29.0 a repeat every so many minutes can run only inside a time window: `between 9:00 and 17:00`, and optionally `on weekdays`, `on weekends`, `on mon,wed`, or `on day`. It runs at the window's start on each day the window opens, then every so many minutes while before the end, which is excluded (9:00, 9:30 … 16:30); a repeat longer than the window runs once, at its start. An end at or before the start ends on the next day, so `between 22:00 and 6:00 on fri` runs from Friday night to Saturday morning; the start and the end cannot be the same. The start and the end are wall-clock times, and the runs between them are real minutes apart: on a night the clocks change, a window that holds the change is an hour shorter or longer, with a run less or more. A server older than 0.29.0 ignores the window and runs such a repeat all day. Each schedule is a durable task: a restart while it waits keeps waiting, one after its time sends at once, and none is ever sent twice. A schedule is cancelled, with a line in the chat saying why, once its person can no longer steer the session or a spend limit is reached. A repeat whose last message still waits in the session's queue (Pi is at work, or a run failed and holds it) sends no other at its next time: the time is skipped, a line in the chat counts the skips, and the Schedules sheet, the Running now board, and Pi's `schedule` tool list show how many there were (since 0.28.1). A repeat can **compact before each run** (a checkbox in its editor, or `compact_before` in Pi's `schedule` tool): before it sends, everything before its last message is summarized, so each run starts on the last run and what was said since, not on all the runs before, which a weekly job would otherwise pay for again each time. It suits a job that keeps its state in files. The first sending has nothing to compact; a session at work when the time comes sends without compacting, and a compaction that fails sends all the same. Limits (settable since 0.24.0): a session holds 50 schedules, a message 4000 characters, a repeat comes at most every 10 minutes (a short repeat can cost money in model calls), and `in …` reaches 366 days ahead. The owner changes each in Menu → Extensions, under Scheduled messages; a change applies at once, to new schedules only. The **Schedules** sheet lists them with a live countdown each; a tap on one opens an editor in its place, to change its message and when it goes out (**Once** on a date and time, **On days** at a time of day, or **Every** so many minutes, hours, or days, with **Only between** for a window and its days) with a preview of the next time, or to delete it, and **New schedule** opens the same editor, beside quick add in words. A change goes out as the work of whoever made it; a new time of day is on their device's clock, and other changes keep the time zone it was set in. **Pause** beside each schedule (and in its editor, and on Running now) stops it at once, without asking Pi: it sends nothing and stays in the list, dashed, until someone taps **Resume**. A repeat then goes on from its next time still to come, and a one-time message whose time passed while paused goes out at once. An edit keeps it paused. The pill and the countdowns count only schedules that go out; a session whose schedules are all paused shows "N paused". Pi's `schedule` tool lists a paused one as paused, and only a person resumes it. Archiving a session that has repeating schedules says how many keep running there, with a tap to see them, and its Schedules sheet marks it as archived; nothing stops or moves (since 0.28.0).
- **Done when.** `/until npm test`: after each of Pi's answers, the check runs in the session's folder; while it fails, Pi gets the end of its output and keeps going, for five checks at most. A bar above the message box shows how it stands. In plan mode the check waits until the plan is approved, and past a spend limit Pi stops going on. A check cut off by a server restart is not run again, as it may have done part of its work: Pi hears so, and its next answer is checked.
- **Mention files.** Type `@` in the message box to pick a file or folder from the session's folder: `@` alone lists its top, `@src/` a folder's own files, and anything else matches across the whole folder (`@srvapp` finds `src/server/app.ts`). Tab or Enter (or a tap) puts the path in, and a folder opens to its files. Pi gets the message as written, `@src/server/app.ts` and all, and reads what it names; or tap **Send them along** above the box to send the files' contents with the message, as Claude Code does (text files up to 100 KB each, images as images when the model takes them). The list is git's in a repository (so ignored files stay out), and a walk that skips hidden and dependency folders elsewhere; your browser fetches it once and matches as you type, so suggestions never wait on the network.
- **Shell commands.** `!git status` runs in the session's folder, and Pi sees the command and what it printed with your next message, as in Pi's terminal app; `!!git status` shows it only to the people here. While it runs, a row says so, with Stop (it is in Running now too). A restart while it runs does not run it again: its entry says it was cut off. A command Pi sees counts as its person's message: Pi works for them from there, as for a steer. These are a person's own commands, not Pi's: plan mode does not hold them back. Stop one from its row, or from Running now on any device.
- **Quiet commands stop (since 0.29.0).** A bash command of Pi's that prints nothing for 15 minutes is stopped with the processes it started, and the call ends as timed out with what it printed, so a hung test run cannot hold a session or a subagent for good. A command that keeps printing, such as a long build, runs on, and Pi can give a call its own `timeout` instead. The owner changes the time in Menu → Extensions, under Background tasks (0: no limit); while Background tasks is off, the time last set there stays in force.
- **Background tasks.** Since 0.24.0, Pi's `background` tool starts a command without waiting for it: a dev server, a watcher, a long build or test run. It runs in the session's folder, reads what the command printed since it last looked (the last 20,000 characters at most), lists the session's tasks and how they ended, and stops one: every process it started, politely, then by force after 4 seconds. A task runs while any process of its group runs, so a command that starts a server in the background and exits keeps its task running. A daemon that leaves the group (a build tool's) does not keep it running: the task ends, says which processes it left, and a stop ends them too. When a task ends by itself, Pi gets a message that starts with `[background]`, with its exit code and the end of its output, as data; Pi can start one without it. Tasks that end together go as one message, which joins the run in flight at its next pause rather than starting another turn (a steer, as a subagent's report is): several ending cost Pi one turn, not one each, and the answer it was writing keeps its place. A task Pi stops is not told about; one a person stops (in Running now) is, as Pi may wait for it (since 0.29.0). A task that prints nothing for 60 minutes is stopped, and Pi is told why; Pi starts a server or a watcher with `quietMinutes: 0` for no limit (since 0.29.0). In the conversation, that message shows as a card each, a group for several: the task's id, how it ended and how long it took, its command, and the last 8 lines of its output as printed, with the rest behind **Show all** (a person who types `[background]` themselves keeps their message as theirs). Each command runs detached from the server, with its output in a log in the data folder (`background/`, in 4 MB segments), so it goes on across a restart from the app; stopping the whole service (systemd) ends it. A durable task watches each one, and after a restart finds the same processes again by their pid, start time, and a marker in their environment. A start is reserved before its process starts, so a tool call run again after a crash never starts the command twice: the watch adopts the processes it finds by their marker; with none left, it tells Pi the exit code the command left, or that how it went cannot be known. A start passes the same hooks as a bash command does, and plan mode blocks it (listing, reading, and stopping stay allowed). Each task shows in Running now, where anyone who may steer the session can stop it. While any runs, the session's status line shows **N in background**, its row in the session list shows a terminal mark with the count, and the rail's Running now button counts the session (since 0.25.0). A tap on that count opens **Background** (Menu → **Background** opens it too, also when nothing runs): the session's commands, the newest first, and what the open one printed, as it comes. It asks again once a second while the task runs, so a dev server's log scrolls by itself; a scroll back up stops it following, and **Jump to the end** goes on. A tap on another command in the row above the log switches to it. **Stop** is there for anyone who may steer. Watching a task takes nothing from Pi: what it reads next stays where it was, so Pi still gets every line it has not read. Past the 20,000 characters the sheet keeps, or past what the log still holds, it says how many bytes it did not show. Limits: 10 running at once in a session (the owner changes it in Menu → Extensions, under Background tasks); a log past 50 MB loses its oldest segments, whole, and a command that writes more than 200 MB in a quarter of a second, or more than 500 MB in 10 seconds, is stopped at once, and Pi is told why; the last 20 ended tasks of a session stay readable. Sessions only: not subagents or PiP. It needs bash and `split`. Since 0.30.0 it runs on Linux, macOS, and Windows (before, Linux only). Each platform knows a process by its pid and its start time together, so a stop never hits a process that took a task's pid later. Linux reads `/proc`, and macOS reads `ps`: a task is its process group, and a process that left the group is found by its marker. Windows has no process groups or signals: a task is its first process and what it starts (by parent and start time), held in a job object while the server runs, through a small PowerShell helper; it needs Git Bash, and a stop ends the processes by force at once. On Windows, a process in a task cannot leave its job, a process started outside it (through a service) is not followed, and a task does not say what it left running ([Windows](windows.md) lists what is weaker there).

- **Watch a pull request.** Menu → **Watch pull request**, `/watch` (or `/watch 123`, `/watch <PR address>`), or Pi's `watch_pr` tool, which it can start after opening a PR (Pi only for its folder's repository, by number; a person for any): until the PR is merged or closed, Pi Pocket looks at it on GitHub every few minutes with the `gh` command signed in on the server, and tells Pi in the session what needs doing, as a message sent through the watch for whoever set it up: checks that failed on its last commit (once all of them finished, with the end of each GitHub Actions log), conflicts with its base branch, and new comments and reviews. Only comments from the repository's owner, and from organization members and collaborators whom GitHub says can write to it, count, and bots' reviews (a member may only read, a collaborator only triage: `gh` asks GitHub, at most every ten minutes for each person, and GitHub tells only an account that can push there, so on a PR where yours cannot, such as a fork's PR to someone else's repository, only the owner's count, and the watch says so); your own account's (which Pi posts as) only when they mention @pi in their own words (not in a quote or code), so Pi never answers itself. Comments keep their lines (a suggested change is code), 30 at most in a message (read newest first, so a burst never hides the new ones); each is told once, and what does not fit in one message comes in the next; nothing goes while Pi is busy there or, with take turns on, while someone else drives (the bar says it waits, and nothing counts as failed); and a watch sends 10 messages at most. It ends, with a line in the chat and a notification, when the PR is merged or closed, when there is more to tell than it may send, after two weeks with nothing new, or after an hour of looks that failed (the bar says why). Logs reach Pi fenced as data, which nothing in them can end early, and titles, check names, and branches come on one line, quoted, as data too. It needs `gh` installed and signed in (`gh auth login`) on the server, on GitHub Enterprise hosts too: a host other than github.com is used only when `gh` is signed in there (`gh auth login --hostname <host>`; a token only in `GH_ENTERPRISE_TOKEN` does not count), for Pi, PiP, and people alike, and Pi names a PR by address only on github.com or its folder's own host.
- **Watch a repository.** Tell PiP in plain words, such as "watch acme/api: when CI fails on main, fix it and tell me" or "when I open an issue labelled pi in acme/web, work on it": it sets up a watch that looks at the repository on GitHub every few minutes with `gh` (no public address needed, unlike a webhook), and what is new there (issues and pull requests opened, CI failing on a branch, releases, someone writing @pi, as a word of its own, in a comment or an issue) comes to PiP, or to a session you name, with your instructions; PiP then acts on it: it starts a session in the repository (its checkout here, or a clone it makes in the data folder's `repos/`), sends it the task, follows it, and tells you what came of it. A watch can be narrowed to a label, to issues assigned to you, or to a branch's CI (mentions of @pi are not narrowed). CI is the branch's own runs: pushed to it, scheduled, started by hand or through the API, or started by another workflow, in the repository itself, never a pull request's or an issue's (a renamed repository keeps working). Only repositories `gh`'s account can push to can be watched or worked on, and issues and pull requests count only from the repository's owner, and organization members and collaborators whom GitHub says can write to it (and bots' pull requests), unless you turn on **From anyone** on the watch in Menu → Webhooks and watches (PiP cannot); @pi always only from them. PiP works in a checkout of a session you started, or in a clone it makes, which appears only once complete (a clone a restart cut off leaves a `~part-…` folder, removed at the next clone of that repository); a different folder already where the clone goes is reported, never overwritten. While Triggers is off, watches wait and ask GitHub nothing; while PiP is off, a watch to PiP holds what it found, and its note says so. What came from GitHub is fenced as data, each thing is told once, ten in a message and twelve messages an hour at most (the rest come next), and a look that fails is tried again, less often each time, with the reason shown. Menu → **Webhooks and watches** lists the watches, to remove one; or ask PiP ("what are you watching?", "stop watching acme/api"). - **Webhooks.** Menu → **Webhooks and watches**: an address that other services (GitHub, a deploy, a monitor) post to, each post becoming a message to Pi in a session, or to PiP, as the work of whoever made the hook: their instructions first ("When a deploy fails, find out why and fix it"), then what was posted, trimmed of noise such as API addresses, and fenced as data. PiP gets them as folded cards and decides what to do: start a session, message one, or tell you. A GitHub hook is signed: GitHub gets the address and, separately, a secret (its Payload URL and Secret), and a post gets in only with GitHub's signature made with it, so whoever sees the address (the repository's admins do, in its settings) cannot post as the hook. Unless a GitHub hook is made **From anyone**, what people outside the repository wrote does not reach Pi (the repository's people are its owner, and the organization members and collaborators whom GitHub says can write to it, asked through `gh`, which must be signed in to the host and able to push there, or else only the owner counts; bots count on pull requests and what is said on them, never on issues): a post an outsider started (their issue, comment, pull request, review, or discussion) is dropped with a 202 that says so; in a post the repository's people started, the body of anything an outsider wrote in it (the issue under a member's comment, a review thread's comments) is left out, its title kept; a workflow run goes only when a branch of the repository itself started it (a push, a schedule, a manual or API dispatch, or another workflow), so one a pull request (a fork's too), an issue, or a comment started is dropped, and so is one whose head repository GitHub does not show; workflow jobs, check suites, and check runs do not say where their run came from, so they are dropped too, as are a commit status on no branch of the repository (a fork's commit) and a security advisory someone reported; and pushes, releases, and other posts that name no author go as they came. A hook for another service has the secret in its address instead: anyone who has the address can message Pi as the hook's person. **New secret** shuts out the old one, for an address or a secret that got out. Anything else gets 404. A post GitHub sends again goes to Pi once, GitHub's ping gets an answer and is nothing for Pi, a hook takes 30 posts an hour of up to 5 MB each (the server reads eight at a time, two to one hook, each within 15 seconds; past that, 503 with Retry-After), a hook to PiP answers 503 while PiP is turned off, and a post that cannot go now (someone else drives, a spend limit) gets a 409 that says no more. Services must be able to reach the server: through a tunnel, a public address works for GitHub. People who can steer make hooks for the sessions they can reach; each sees their own, the owner every one. Choose **Edit prompt** to change a hook's instructions, then **Save** or **Cancel**. Only the hook's creator or the server owner can save changes, and they need steering rights. You can enter up to 2000 characters. Leave the instructions empty to ask Pi to summarize what happened and whether anything needs doing. Saved changes apply to later posts. The address, secret, destination, and usage history stay unchanged. Webhooks and the Relay (the next item) deliver the same way, through core's inbox: the same fence, the same once-only key, and a limit for each source. The watch and webhooks are the **Triggers** extension: turned off, watches wait and hooks answer 503.
- **Relay.** Menu → **Extensions** → **Relay** (off by default) lets a chat service or a feed send messages to a session, with no separate relay service and no public address: each connector makes outbound connections only (it polls, or keeps a stream open), and delivers inside the server. Menu → **Relay** (the owner only) adds a connector: choose its kind, a session, and instructions for Pi, then its fields. **Discord** needs a bot token, and the bot's **Message Content Intent** turned on in the Developer Portal; **Slack** uses Socket Mode, with an app token (`xapp-…`) and a bot token (`xoxb-…`); **Telegram** needs a bot token; **Matrix** needs a homeserver address and an access token; **RSS** needs a feed address; **HTTP** polls any JSON address, with headers, the fields of an item, and an optional reply address. For a chat service, give channel, chat, or room IDs, or leave them empty for all the bot can see; for direct messages, Discord has its own switch. Each message comes to the session as `[via relay <name>]`: the owner's instructions first, then the sender, then what was written, fenced as data in `<relay-message>` tags. Pi answers with the `relay_reply` tool, which only a session that a connector delivers to is offered; a reply goes to where the message came from, and a call that runs again sends once. On **Discord**, **Slack**, **Telegram**, and **Matrix**, Pi can send images and files from the session's folder with a reply (10 files; 10 MB each on Discord, 50 MB on the others, 100 MB in all; never a hidden file, a `.env` file such as `prod.env`, a file outside the folder, or the server's data), and can download an attachment that a person posted with `relay_fetch`, into `.relay/` in the session's folder, where it reads an image (25 MB at most, 20 MB on Telegram); the token and the file's link never reach Pi. Slack needs the `files:write` and `files:read` scopes for this, and Matrix fetches nothing from an encrypted room. Each event is delivered once, also across restarts: what came waits on disk until the session takes it (a busy session, a spend limit, or a restart do not lose it). Messages from bots, and from the connector's own account, are dropped. **Mention only** takes messages that name the bot, or that come in a direct message. **Owner IDs** are your own accounts on the service: Pi reads them as an id you marked as trusted (guidance for the model, not a boundary, since some services let a person pretend to be another), and they have no limit; every other author has 10 messages an hour by default, and a connector 30 an hour (both can be changed; a feed's items, RSS, or HTTP without an author id field, have only the connector's limit), and a message over a limit is dropped and counted. The first look at a feed or an address takes nothing that was there before. Tokens are kept on the server only (a file of mode 0600 in the data folder's `x.pocket-relay`, which Pi's tools cannot read), never reach the browser (it only shows that one is set), and are hidden from transcripts and errors. A connector that fails shows why and tries again, less often each time; a refused token stops it until you edit it, and an error never stops the server. Drop-in extensions can add kinds of connectors ([Relay connectors](extensions/relay.md)).

- **Artifacts.** The `artifact` tool publishes versioned HTML pages, Markdown documents (shown in the app's theme), or SVG images. Give it `content`, `edits` against the latest version, or a `path` to a UTF-8 text file on the server (up to 2 MB). A path can be absolute, start with `~/`, or be relative to the session's folder. The file is copied into a durable version, not linked: changing or deleting the source does not change the artifact. For a new artifact, `.html`, `.md`/`.markdown`, and `.svg` infer its type; a path update keeps the existing id's type. A page can carry a bundle of files (since 0.26.0). `assets` adds up to 200 entries, each `{ name, path }` (a file on the server, binary allowed, copied and not linked) or `{ name, content }` (UTF-8 text): images, fonts, stylesheets, scripts, data files, or more HTML pages. A name is a relative path of letters, digits, dots, underscores, and hyphens, up to 200 characters, with no `.` or `..` segment. A new version keeps the assets of the one before it; an entry with the same name replaces one, and `removeAssets` drops some. One asset is up to 5 MB, and one version's assets up to 20 MB. A relative URL in the page (`assets/logo.png`, `about.html`) loads from the bundle in the viewer, in an inline card after Run, in the artifact's own tab, and in a published link. Each file is sent with the sandbox's policy, `nosniff`, and a type from its extension (an unknown one is never HTML). Assets are stored once for each content hash. A private page reaches its assets through a signed address that lasts 12 hours, so a page left open longer loads new assets only after a reload. `group` (a lowercase slug) puts artifacts together as variations of one design, and `kind` (`page` or `deck`) says what a design is; Design uses both. Artifacts from before 0.26.0 work as they did. `inline: true` (since 0.28.0, one tool; before it a second tool, `artifact_inline`, sent almost the same schema again on every request) is the same store for HTML only (`content` or `edits`, no path, no `type` but `html`): the call shows as a card in the reply, between Pi's words, instead of a link (see Rich replies). Pi's guide says when to use which: the card for a small interactive piece that belongs to what it is explaining, the viewer for a whole page, a game, a Markdown document, or an SVG image. An id made by either tool takes versions from both, but an `inline: true` call refuses an id that is Markdown or SVG, and the `artifact` tool cannot change the type of an id that has had an inline version (a card names a version, and the page comes by the artifact's type): Pi uses a new id for that. Pi's guide mentions `inline` only in sessions whose `artifact` tool has it (PiP's has not), and says that only a direct call shows a card. Sessions from before 0.28.0 keep their `artifact_inline` calls and results as stored, and still show them as cards, also in forks; the model is shown those calls as `artifact` with `inline: true` in its requests, so it does not repeat a tool it is no longer offered. The Artifacts sheet lists every version in a rounded card grid; opening one shows the sandboxed viewer (or its own tab). The list does not run artifacts as previews. Artifacts cannot reach the app or its cookies. In the viewer, an HTML artifact can ask for two things, which Pi knows how to build: a **follow-up**, a message it puts in your message box (after your draft, starting with which artifact it is from, with any selection as JSON) for you to read and send, never sent by itself, and only after you tap the page (a real tap or key press in the page, up to 5 seconds before: the page cannot fake one, and `window.focus()` does not count; each tap serves one follow-up), at most every 2 seconds (the viewer then closes, so the box shows); and **saved state**, up to 16 KB of JSON that it saves for you alone, so its filters and inputs come back after a reload, a restart, or on another device. Saved state stays out of Pi's context, belongs to the artifact rather than a version, and starts empty in a fork; a page that changes the state's shape names a new schema, and the old state then reads as none. Only people who can write to Pi get a follow-up; a viewer gets a notice. HTML run from a reply (▶ Run) can offer a follow-up too, but keeps no state. Only the artifact's first page is heard: as it starts, it opens a private channel to the app (a script the server puts first in an HTML artifact's page when a frame asks for it, and in the frame page that runs a reply's HTML), and everything goes over that channel, so a page the frame goes on to cannot use it and an answer still on its way never reaches it. `schema` is a string (a number is refused, for a get and a set alike). The bridge is for HTML artifacts, not Markdown or SVG. Neither works in a published link or an artifact's own tab. A session keeps up to 300 saved values; a person removed from the app keeps theirs until the session goes. **Share** in the viewer publishes one artifact to anyone with the link (`/pub/<token>`), with no sign-in: the page is the artifact's own sandboxed one, so it still cannot reach the app, and its address is the whole of the access. A bundle's files are at `/pub/<token>/<name>`, checked on every request: ending or expiring the link closes every file, a link that follows the newest version follows its assets, and a pinned link keeps its own. A published page gets no bridge. A share either stays at the version it was made from or follows the artifact's newest, and either lasts until it is ended or closes after a day, a week, or a month; **End** closes it at once, and the address then answers nothing. Only people who steer and see every session publish or end one, or see its address, never viewers or someone invited to a single session. The sheet lists the session's links still in force, with the address to copy; it starts at the server's public address when a tunnel gives one, else at this browser's. **Delete** (the bin in the viewer, and on each card of the Artifacts sheet, since 0.30.0) takes an artifact with all its versions, after a confirm: its pages, its share links, the state its page saved, and its design comments go in one commit, so a restart finds it all there or all gone, and the storage drops the older revisions that held them. Its asset files go too once no artifact in any session uses them (a sweep after the delete, run again at startup if a restart cut it off). Cards in replies say it was deleted, also after a reload (the session's view carries the last number of each deleted artifact). A share or saved state made while it is deleted is refused, and edits that Pi sent for a deleted version are refused too. What stays is Pi's own messages: the tool calls that made it still hold the text Pi sent, and the confirm says so. Anyone who steers the session may delete, never a viewer; Pi has no tool to delete. One version alone cannot be deleted, as cards in replies and design comments name versions by number. A fork keeps its own copy. Published again under the same id, it starts at the next number, never one a card names.

- **Waiting out provider limits.** When a provider's limit stops a run (a 429 or an overload, a Claude or ChatGPT usage window, a model's cap), Pi Pocket waits for it to clear and goes on by itself, instead of failing. Tap it (or the count in the top bar while one works) for a row each: what it was asked, what it does this moment (the call it runs, or what it writes), for how long, the model and thinking level it runs on (with ↗ when that is not the session's model), whether its report is on its way, **Stop**, and a tap to open it; a call waiting for approval says so, and a failed one says why, and "dismissed" once someone dealt with it (see Subagents board). Done, stopped, and failed ones are counted apart, failed ones first. Once none works it stays until you put it away (×), and comes back, counting only the new ones, when one starts again. Viewers see it too, without Stop.
- **Subagents board.** The rail's ⑂ shows every subagent in every session in the conversation's place: a soft state-colored square each, in a rounded row per session, those that need you first, and under each row a sub-tile for every subagent at work there, with its state and the model it runs on, so a glance says what each is doing without opening one (a tap picks it for the detail card, staying on the board; a double click, or double tap, opens it). A row shows six sub-tiles (three on a phone), and the selected one wherever it is; **Show N more** shows the rest. Working squares pulse; the selected square has a ring, and filtered-out squares fade. A proportional state bar and filter pills with counts say how many wait for an approval, failed, work, were stopped, or are done, and let you filter by one; a search finds a subagent by its name, what it was asked, what it does now, or why it failed. **Needs you** lists those waiting for an approval and those that failed with nobody dealing with it yet. The selected subagent's detail card shows its session, how long it has worked (or when it ended), what it was asked and does now, and its error, with **Open** (**Review the approval** while it waits), **Stop**, and, for a failure, **Dismiss** (off while it is asked; when the subagent failed again since, a notice says so and the newer failure stays). A failure is dealt with once someone who can steer dismisses it, or opens the subagent from the board, its row in the subagents bar, its report or the tool call that started it, or the session menu: it stays on the board as failed ("Failed · dismissed", without the "!"), and leaves Needs you, the rail's red mark, and the session's red ⑂, for everyone, after a reload or a restart too. A message someone sends it, like later work from Pi, settles it by what it comes to, after a restart too: answered, the subagent is done; failing again, it needs someone again. The session's activity says who dealt with a failure, whichever way. Hover a square to see what it does in the line under them; the arrows move the focus between squares and Enter opens the one focused; Esc, back, or × closes it, and on a phone a swipe right does too. Archived sessions show only while a subagent works there. A session keeps its 24 newest finished squares (done, stopped, or failed and dealt with); older ones are counted, as **+N** after its squares and in the bar's numbers. On a phone it is one column, Needs you first, with bigger squares and a bar at the bottom for the one selected.
- **The queue and the bars above the message box.** Messages waiting for Pi show above the message box, with how many wait whenever not all of them show. With the bars there (the subagents bar, plan mode, a goal, a PR watch, a trust question, who drives), they take less than half of the room they share with the conversation when the bars fit in it, and the conversation keeps the rest, staying where you were reading. The bars always show whole; the queue and the subagents' list take what the bars leave, and scroll. Unfolding the subagents bar gives the list the room, one whole row at least: the queue shows fewer rows, or folds to how many wait. With several bars on a short screen, the plan, goal, watch, and trust bars keep one line each, their words cut short and their buttons whole. When even that does not fit (a phone on its side with every bar up), the stack scrolls, and the conversation keeps a quarter of the room, or a few lines, whichever is less.
- **Live editing.** No build step. Edit a file in `web/` and every open browser reloads. Edit a file in `src/server/extensions/` and it is reinstalled into the running server (a running tool call finishes on the old code). For core server changes, use **Restart server** in the menu: running work resumes after the restart.
- **Core's secrets stay out of transcripts.** These secrets are hidden in every session, PiP and subagents included, as a value an extension hides with `host.hide` is: each module's `secret: true` settings (and their defaults), the owner token, the private key in `push.json`, and the keys and tokens in Pi's `auth.json`. The secrets Pi Pocket keeps in `pocket.sqlite` are not hidden, and nothing keeps Pi from that file: a webhook's secret, an artifact's share token, and a public link's token. Each shows as its label, such as `[secret vault.apiKey]` or `[owner token]`, in commands' output, every tool's result, and people's messages. Core reads them again when the owner saves settings, and before each command and after each tool when a file changed; a value that changes stays hidden as the old one too, while the server runs. A module that is off has its values in `config.json` too: which are secret is unknown then, so none is hidden, except a value hidden while it was on, which stays hidden while the server runs. Hiding is by value: a command that encodes or splits a value on purpose shows it.
- **Codemode.** On by default, like `"+codemode"` in Pi's `defaultTools`. The `codemode` tool runs JavaScript the agent writes in a QuickJS sandbox with no Node, files, network, or timers; its only capability is calling the agent's other tools as `tools.<name>(args)`. Use it to batch independent calls, chain them, or filter large output before it reaches the model. Each call a script makes goes through the same checks and hooks as a direct call, so a hook that blocks or asks for approval sees it. `store()` and `load()` keep values per conversation. Its card shows the script, each call it made, and the output. Since 0.28.0, Codemode has a **Mode** setting in Menu → Extensions, for the owner and all sessions, as Pi's `codemode.mode`. **On** (the default) changes nothing. **Only** declares only `codemode`, `tool_search`, `artifact`, `ask`, and `memory` (the cards of the last 3 need a direct call). Pi calls every other tool only from scripts (except a drop-in tool whose `callers` names `"model"`), also the tools of a module that is turned on later, and a tool that a session loaded before. The codemode section of the prompt lists each of these tools with its description and parameters, up to 3,000 tokens (Pi's default `codemode.inlineBudget`), and then the names of the others. Tools that wait for `tool_search` still wait, and a tool that it loads goes through codemode. A subagent that gets one of these tools also gets `codemode`. One limit: a subagent started in On mode with a list of tools without `codemode` cannot call a tool in Only mode, because the setting cannot see each session's list.
- **Share to Pi.** With Pi Pocket on the home screen (Android), share a link, some text, or a screenshot from any app to Pi Pocket: pick a session or a new one, and it goes into the message box with the files attached, ready to send.
- **Pi's questions.** Since 0.27.0, Pi's `ask` tool asks the person it works for 1 to 4 questions on a card at its call, when a decision changes the work and only they can make it. Each question has a short tag, the question, and 2 to 4 answers as buttons, each with a line under it; a tap chooses one (or several, where the question allows it), and **Or type your own answer** takes a typed one instead. **Send** is on once each question has an answer; **Decline** answers nothing, and Pi goes on with its best judgment. Pi waits for the answer, for hours if it must, and after a restart the same card asks again. Pi gets one line per question (`Storage: Local storage`), and the call shows each question and its answer to everyone in the session. The card shows, and notifies, as an extension's question does (below). It runs in plan mode, the guard never asks about it, and subagents do not have it. The **Ask** module, on by default (Menu → Extensions).
- **Pi's reactions.** Pi's `react` tool puts an emoji on the person's newest message in the session (up to 3 of Pi's own on one message), or takes one off: a quick sign that it got the message while the work comes before its answer, which it takes off once it answers. Each shows as a small pill under that message, for everyone who has the session open, and live as it changes. It takes the emoji itself, never a word; it runs in plan mode, and subagents cannot use it. The **Reactions** module, on by default (Menu → Extensions).
- **Questions from extensions.** An extension can ask the person Pi works for on a card, as an approval asks: a sign-in, a one-time code, a key, or "solve the CAPTCHA, then tap Done". Only that person can fill it in (the owner, when nobody is known); anyone who can steer can **Decline**. What is typed goes to the extension, never to Pi, into the transcript, or to the other people here, and the call's line says **Answered** or **Declined** by whom. A notification goes to that person alone, and opens the session: the fields are filled in in the app. Extensions can also hide values (they show as `[label]` in command output, tool results, and in a message to Pi that someone pasted one into), give commands environment variables, and use the session's browser page. Since 0.22.0 ([docs/extensions/people-values.md](extensions/people-values.md)).
- **Docs for agents.** Pi's system prompt says where Pi Pocket's documentation is ([index.md](index.md)), to read when someone asks about Pi Pocket itself: changing how Pi works without code, writing extensions (with tested examples), and changing Pi Pocket's code while it runs, checked first on a throwaway copy (`node test/serve.ts`).
- **Drop-in extensions.** Put your own extension module (a `.ts` file that default-exports `(host, web) => Extension`, or a list, or nothing) in `~/.pi-pocket/extensions/`. It shows in the Extensions sheet, stays off until the owner turns it on, can import Pi Pocket's packages (Pi Durable, pi-ai, Pi's MCP client pi-mcp, and the rest) as the built-in modules do, and reloads when edited. One that would take the name of a built-in extension or tool, or of another drop-in's, is not loaded, and the sheet says why: installing it would replace that one.
- **Extensions in the app.** A module can add to the app itself, without changes to Pi Pocket's code: routes on the server, commands for the message box, and a browser module with buttons on the rail, pages in the conversation's place (as the subagents board shows), panels beside the conversation (where the Browser and Files panels dock, with a button in the top bar and the places), items in the status line under the message box (with a short form a narrow line takes), sheets, rows in the menu, and launcher actions, which work without asking Pi. A dashboard of a provider's usage, the server's load, or a machine's power and temperature, say: the route asks the service with a key that stays on the server, and the page shows what it says, live with the module's events. It is the owner's alone unless the module opens it to people who can steer, or to everyone who sees every session; someone invited to one session never gets it. A module can also show **public pages** to anyone with one of its links, with no sign-in (`/l/<token>`, since 0.22.0): each link closes at its time (30 days at most) or after the answers it takes, the page runs in a sandbox with no script, so whoever opens it while signed in lends it nothing, and Extensions says which modules have them ([docs/extensions/public-pages.md](extensions/public-pages.md)). Turning the module off takes away what it added, its pages included; an edit swaps the routes, and an edit to the browser module's files has tabs load it again. One that fails tells the owner why in a notice and in the Extensions sheet, and the rest of the app goes on; a tab opened with `?noext=1` loads none of their browser parts (safe mode), to turn off one that breaks the app. [docs/extensions.md](extensions/app.md) says how, with an example: a page with the server's load, memory, and uptime.
- **Extension workspace APIs (0.16.0).** A page can save its own durable catalog with request-lifetime document commits, or use a source-bound route to start linked sessions as the person who clicked Run. Current source access, turn, plan, spend, team, and model checks apply; explicit thinking choices are not silently lowered. Human history can open authorized sessions through `pocket.openConversation`. Extensions can read the running server's package version through `host.pocketVersion` or browser `pocket.pocketVersion`, without an updater or restart control; the extension API level remains 1.
- **Extensions hub.** Menu → **Extensions** opens **Discover**, **Installed**, and **Updates**, as tabs in the sheet's head, for the owner while the gallery is on. Wide screens show the list and the selected entry side by side; phones show one at a time, with Back returning to the filtered list. The list is in sections: **Extensions** as dense rows divided by hairlines (a small mark, the name and its maker, one line about it, and up to three facts of what it reaches or adds, such as `openrouter.ai`, `a key`, `sees turns`, or `2 tools`), and **Themes** as cards, each a tiny desktop drawn in the theme's own colors with its color dots (since 0.27.0). Filters narrow Discover to **Extensions** or **Themes**, and to those with **Tools for Pi**, a part **In the app**, or ones that **Change Pi** (hooks, prompt sections, wraps). **Installed** (also tap "guard" in the status line) lists the **Built in** modules first, open, with how many are on; then your **Extensions** (from the gallery and your own drop-ins), each with how it stands now: **On**, **Off**, **Did not load** (with the error under it), **Off in this tab** (its browser part failed here), **Edited since install**, or **Revoked**; then your **Themes** (from the gallery, and your own, marked **Yours**, which open Appearance). A module's row shows its tools, the routes, commands, and browser module it adds to the app, and any load error, in this browser too. The owner can turn Artifacts, Design, Browser, Subagents, Scheduled messages, Done when, Plan mode, Codemode, Memory, PiP, Triggers, Extension gallery, Pi Pocket updates, Agent Teams, or a drop-in off and on, or reload one; the change applies to every session at once, stays after restarts (`disabledExtensions` and `enabledExtensions` in `~/.pi-pocket/config.json`), and everyone in a session sees who changed what. This leaves Pi's own setting (`~/.pi/lancet-guard.json`) alone. Under PiP, the owner picks when PiP compacts its context. The system prompt cannot be turned off.
- **Extension settings.** A module can declare settings (on or off, text, a number, a choice, or a secret such as an API key); the owner changes them in Menu → Extensions under the module, with **Save settings**, and **Default** or **Clear** to put one back. A secret's value never leaves the server: the sheet shows only that it is set. Modules can also listen to what happens in the app (sessions made, renamed, and archived, messages, runs) and offer services to each other; Extensions says which ones offer a service. Since 0.22.0 ([docs/extensions/services-settings.md](extensions/services-settings.md)).
- **OpenAI-compatible endpoints.** The owner adds an endpoint from the **OpenAI Compatible** row of the Providers list (**Add**; searching "ollama", "local", or "custom" finds it): a name, a base URL (`http://localhost:1234/v1`), and an API key, or **No key** for a local server. **Find models** asks the endpoint's `GET /models` and lists what it has, each with a checkbox; a model ID can also be typed by hand, and either way it is added. Each chosen model has an **Images** checkbox (it sees images: Pi sends them instead of a placeholder) and a **Thinking** button that opens its thinking levels: on, it starts with Low, Medium, High, Extra high, and Max, each sending its own name as the reasoning effort; levels can be added (**Add level** adds the next one above the highest, below the last row; Off and Minimal too), removed, or sent under another name (a model whose top effort is `hard`). The rows stay in Pi's order, from Off to Max, as the model menu lists them; a row's menu changes which level it is. A **Context & price** button opens its context window and maximum output, in tokens (empty: Pi's defaults, 128k and 16k), and its price in dollars per million tokens: input, output, cache read, and cache write (empty: the model costs nothing; a cache price left empty is 0). The context meter and compaction go by the context window; each request asks for up to the maximum output, so it must be at or below what the endpoint allows; each message's cost, and spend limits, go by the price. All of these are set by hand and saved in `models.json`, and **Edit** shows them as saved; Pi Pocket does not guess them from the endpoint's list, which has no common format for them. The one exception is a vLLM server's `max_model_len`, which fills in the context window of a model chosen from its list. **Save** writes the endpoint into Pi's `~/.pi/agent/models.json` (made, with `~/.pi/agent/`, when missing) and keeps the key in Pi's sign-ins (`auth.json`), so its models show in the model menu at once, with no restart. Each endpoint is a row of the list like any provider, with **Edit** (an empty key keeps the saved one; models can be found again with the saved key, at the same address only) and **Remove** (which removes its key too). Pi Pocket rewrites `models.json` only when it is plain JSON that Pi accepts, and keeps the fields that it does not set; a file with comments or trailing commas is left alone, with a message.
- **Refresh models.** The owner can tap **Refresh models** in the menu (under App) or the Providers sheet (the refresh button beside the Subagent models heading), or run **Refresh models** from the Ctrl/⌘+K launcher. The same controls work on phones. Refresh reloads `models.json` and Pi’s sign-ins, and asks configured providers for fresh catalogs. The menu's row says when it is refreshing, and a notice gives the added and removed model counts, without closing the menu; the Providers sheet also shows the errors for individual providers. All connected tabs receive the updated list. Healthy providers finish independently of slow ones; each provider has an eight-second limit. Running sessions keep their chosen models. A local model must be configured in `models.json` or registered by a Pocket-compatible extension: refresh does not load regular Pi CLI extensions or discover arbitrary local servers.
- **Uploads.** In normal chat and PiP, attach files with the picker, paste files or images, or drop them into the message box. Files are saved on the server for the conversation you send to and their paths are given to the agent; supported images also go straight to models that accept them. That includes HEIC and HEIF photos (from an iPhone, say), which the server converts to JPEG for the model in a worker, one photo at a time, with limits of 30 seconds and 64 million pixels (a photo that big needs about 1 GB of memory while it converts), so a broken or huge photo gets a notice and does not hold up the server; the original stays in the session's uploads. When the image the model gets differs from the file, its line in the message says so: the format when it changes (`[Image: shown to you as JPEG.]`, always for a HEIC photo), and, in the words of Pi's `read` tool, the original size, the shown size, and the multiplier when it is made smaller. An image that does not change gets no note. A sent HEIC photo shows in the chat and in PiP as the JPEG the model got; when the model did not get one (a model without images, or a failed conversion), or when Pi Pocket cannot be sure which image is the photo's (the message names an image with `@`, or another attached image failed), it shows as a file. An image starts to be made ready for the model as soon as it uploads, so a send seldom waits for it. In a session's message box (not PiP's), attached files stay across a reload, as the draft does, and leave with your sign-out; while a message is on its way, the box dims and holds what it sends, its words, files, and quote, which no key, dictation, or × changes until it went (in PiP's box too, which empties as it sends), also when it shows again meanwhile (you went to another place and came back), so it cannot go twice; text sent to the box meanwhile (Send to Pi) goes in once it went or failed, and so does a queued message taken back with Edit, kept meanwhile in the browser, also through a reload or a move; the same words and files sent again after a lost answer, also after a reload, find the message that went instead of making a second; once you change them, or after an answer that said it was refused or failed, they go again; if the send fails, it is all there to send again, with its quote.

A span closes with as many backticks as opened it, a fence's lines stand on their own, and a stray or unclosed one stays text. Nothing else is formatted: `*`, `#`, lists, and spacing stay as typed, and @mentions outside code stay buttons. Only the display changes: Pi, exports, and ↑ get the message as written.

Some show more than code, with the code a tab away: an `html` block (or a reply that is a whole page) is previewed as a page, inline, with its scripts off and nothing loaded from anywhere; **▶ Run** runs its scripts in the artifacts' sandbox, where it can reach the internet but not the app; an `svg` block is drawn as an image (tap it for full screen); and a `diff` or `patch` block opens in the diff viewer. While Pi is still writing, they show as code. Write cards color the file they write, and the file viewer previews `.html` files the same way, with Run. **Cards in the reply.** An `artifact` call with `inline: true` (an `artifact_inline` call, in a session from before 0.28.0) shows as a card in the reply, as the `html` block above with the artifact's name and version in its bar, an **Open** button for the artifact viewer at that version, and the same Preview/Code and Run/Stop; a failed, denied, or stopped call shows as a step with its reason instead. A card's page is read from the artifact's own address, so whoever can see the session sees the card, a viewer and someone invited to this session alone included, and each can Run it. Once run, its page gets what an artifact's does: a follow-up for the message box (a notice for a viewer) and saved state under the artifact's id, shared with the viewer. A preview grows to fit the page, up to 1200 px, and a running page up to 900 px, then scrolls inside. On a phone the bar takes two lines. ## Limitations

- Pi Durable is experimental, and its API can change between releases. Versions are pinned in `package.json`.
- Only one server process can use the database at a time.
- Forks are separate sessions; there is no branch tree to switch between inside one session.
- Pi's own extensions are not supported: Pi Pocket runs its own extensions on Pi Durable, and drop-ins from `~/.pi-pocket/extensions/`. Pi's prompt templates work as slash commands.
- A git worktree keeps a session's files apart; it is not a sandbox. Pi can still reach everything you can.
- Artifacts run in an opaque-origin sandbox, so `localStorage` and cookies are unavailable inside them. In the viewer, they can save up to 16 KB of state for each person through the app instead.
- The browser needs Chromium, Chrome, Brave, or Edge on the server. Its pages live in memory: a restart opens each one again at its last address, but not what was typed into it. Each session's cookies are its own and last until the page closes. A browser installed as a snap (Ubuntu's Chromium) is used only when there is no other; it cannot open files outside your home folder. The list of servers running on the machine is Linux-only. Tested on Linux and macOS; not yet on Windows.
